The hands-on challenge

The business challenge: What happens when you delegate real work to AI?

Imagine you are a junior business analyst helping a company prepare its weekly sales reports. The reports contain useful numbers, but identifying emerging sales problems and deciding what deserves attention still takes considerable analytical effort.

What if you could delegate not just the reporting task, but the responsibility for discovering better ways to identify emerging business problems?

How much of this work would you delegate to an AI agent—and what decisions would you allow it to make?

In this exercise, you will use Codex to explore progressively greater levels of responsibility and authority delegated to an AI agent. You will begin by asking it to analyze and recommend, then delegate the production of a management briefing, and finally give it a broader business objective to pursue.

The business opportunity: Move beyond using AI for advice toward delegating meaningful business work—while retaining control over what the AI is responsible for, what it may do, and how its performance is evaluated.

The framework lens

Understanding the AI Agency Maturity Framework

The AI Agency Maturity Framework helps explain what changes across these exercises. It describes increasing levels of responsibility and authority delegated to AI systems—not simply increasing technical sophistication. You will explore the shift from AI that advises, to AI that completes a task, to AI that pursues an operational objective.

The framework has six dimensions, grouped around three important management concepts:

Directive — Responsibility

  • What goal or outcome is the AI responsible for achieving, within what scope and constraints?

Agency — Authority

  • What decisions and actions is the AI authorized to undertake?

Supporting Capabilities

  • Context: What information and environment are available?
  • Reasoning: What must the AI figure out?
  • Experimentation: What alternatives can the AI deliberately test?
  • Learning: What can the AI learn from results and carry forward?

Effective AI delegation requires alignment among responsibility, authority, and capability. The goal is not maximum autonomy, but the right level for the business task and its risks.

The critical distinction: As responsibility expands from completing tasks to pursuing objectives to improving operations, corresponding authority expands from selecting and executing actions to coordinating work to changing the operation itself.

Key takeaway: Greater responsibility requires appropriately aligned authority—not simply more sophisticated AI capabilities.

Stage 5 connection: The objective is earlier detection of sales deterioration; the policy is the rule governing alerts; the deliverable is a revised management briefing incorporating the selected policy. Changing report content alone does not establish Stage 5 autonomy.

Important: Codex is the agent. The sales-reporting application is conventional software; this exercise does not turn it into an AI agent. The final exercise illustrates Stage 5-oriented delegation and experimentation, not proof of full Stage 5 maturity across all six dimensions.

Management perspective

7. Designing Governance, Risk, and Accountability

As organizations delegate increasing responsibility and authority to AI agents, managers must design the governance framework within which those agents operate. This is a managerial responsibility, not primarily a technical implementation task.

The manager's role is not to manage every action an AI agent takes, but to establish the responsibilities, decision rights, risk boundaries, and accountability mechanisms that govern its operation.

Enduring principle: Responsibility and authority can be delegated to AI. Accountability cannot.

People and organizations that authorize and govern AI remain accountable for that delegation. The person entering a prompt may bear responsibility, but is not necessarily the only accountable party.

Your management challenge

Imagine you are a team lead introducing agents like Codex at a company. What rules and safeguards would you establish before using them with real business data?

Addendum / The working environment

Where ChatGPT, Cowork, and Codex fit

These are not three unrelated kinds of AI. They are different ways of working with AI, with some overlap. The distinction is the environment the AI can operate in, the responsibility it is given, and how work is executed and supervised.

Explore and create

ChatGPT Chat

Use conventional chat to understand a concept, brainstorm, develop a strategy, draft a document, or ask for code. It is particularly effective when the main output is an answer, explanation, or proposed solution.

Depending on available tools and connections, chat can also take actions; it is not limited to text generation.

Delegate complex knowledge work

ChatGPT Work

Work supports longer, more involved tasks across files, research, connected applications, and deliverables such as documents, spreadsheets, presentations, and reports.

It brings the agentic approach into a broader knowledge-work environment and may support scheduled or trigger-based workflows, depending on available features.

Delegate software work

Codex

Codex specializes in software projects, repositories, terminals, and developer tools. It can inspect a codebase, change files, run code and tests, investigate failures, revise its implementation, and present changes for review.

Its environment is particularly suited to iterative software development and parallel coding tasks.

Is ChatGPT Work another way to implement agentic workflows?

ChatGPT Work brings the agentic approach into general knowledge work. It can be delegated multi-step tasks and broader objectives, use connected tools and files, coordinate activities, and produce finished business deliverables.

These capabilities make ChatGPT Work particularly relevant to Stage 5-oriented AI agency, where the system determines what work is needed to pursue an objective rather than simply executing a prescribed task.

Codex applies the same broad agentic approach particularly to software development, where it can work directly with code, run commands and tests, and revise its implementation.

Importantly, neither ChatGPT Work nor Codex is inherently Stage 4 or Stage 5. Maturity depends on the responsibility delegated (Directive), the decisions and actions authorized (Agency), and the supporting capabilities.

Addendum 2 / From one-off tasks to continuous experimentation

Karpathy’s Loop: Toward a Perpetual Agentic System

In Autoresearch, AI researcher Andrej Karpathy demonstrates a simple but powerful pattern: an AI coding agent repeatedly proposes a change, runs an experiment, measures whether the result improved, keeps or rejects the change, and tries again. Instead of finishing after one successful task, the agent can continue exploring improvements across many cycles.

How the loop works

1ProposeChoose a hypothesis or improvement
→
2Change & runImplement and test the idea
→
3EvaluateMeasure against a fixed criterion
→
4Keep or revertRetain gains; reject regressions
↻ Repeat: use the outcome of one experiment to inform the next.

Karpathy’s original example uses a small language-model training project. The agent edits a designated training file, runs a five-minute experiment, compares a validation metric, and repeats. A human-written program.md defines the experimental instructions and boundaries. The key innovation is the repeatable decision-and-experiment cycle, not a particular AI model or programming language.

How this could apply to our sales-reporting exercise

In Exercise 3, Codex compares alternative methods for detecting sales deterioration. A loop-based version could repeatedly:

  1. Propose a new alerting rule or a modification to the current method.
  2. Evaluate it on historical sales data, using chronological backtesting.
  3. Measure detection effectiveness, false alarms, and alert volume.
  4. Keep an improvement only if it satisfies pre-established business criteria and guardrails; otherwise revert it.
  5. Record the result and start another experiment.
From a task to a perpetual process: Exercise 3 ends after Codex evaluates competing policies, recommends an approach, and demonstrates its application in a revised management briefing. It does not activate an ongoing alerting process. A continuously running loop can initiate the next experiment without a new student prompt each time. In practice, this requires a persistent execution mechanism (such as an orchestrator or scheduled worker), durable experiment records, and an explicit stopping or pause policy. A single Codex prompt does not guarantee indefinite operation.

What makes a perpetual loop useful—and what can go wrong?

Potential benefitNecessary safeguard
More experiments with less manual interventionLimit time, cost, number of experiments, and permitted file changes.
Improvements accumulate across cyclesKeep versioned results and a reliable rollback mechanism.
Decisions become evidence-drivenUse meaningful business measures, out-of-sample evaluation, and checks against overfitting.
Agent can continue without constant supervisionDefine approval thresholds, monitoring, audit logs, and a human stop control.

Connection to AI Agency Maturity: The loop strengthens Experimentation and can support Learning when results are retained and influence future attempts. But repetition alone does not establish full Stage 5 maturity across Directive, Agency, Context, Reasoning, Experimentation, and Learning. Nor does retaining a better rule imply the underlying language model has retrained itself.

Think about it

If this sales-reporting agent were allowed to run continuously, what should it be permitted to change on its own, what evidence would justify keeping a change, and when should a human intervene?
