Activation Functions: From Linear to Nonlinear

Discover why neural networks need nonlinearity, then experiment with weights and bias to shape activation patterns.

Learning objective

Demonstrate how a nonlinear activation function transforms a straight-line pattern of pre-activation values into a nonlinear pattern of activation values.

1. Why do we need activation functions?

Start here

Linear (or affine) transformations can stretch, compress, rotate, and shear a representation space, but they cannot bend or fold it. Nonlinear activation functions enable neural networks to learn transformations that can bend and fold a representation space, making complex patterns easier to model and classes easier to separate.

Think of the representation space as a flexible sheet, with observations resting on it. When the sheet is reshaped, the observations move with it. Their new coordinates—and potentially their distances and relationships to other observations—change. The observations themselves have not changed; their representations have.

An affine transformation stretches, compresses, rotates, or shears the sheet without bending it. A nonlinear transformation can deform different regions differently, allowing the sheet to bend or fold. This is an analogy: real neural networks map numerical representations and may also change their dimensionality; they do not literally move a physical surface.

What will you discover?

In this exercise, you will see how a nonlinear activation function can transform a straight-line sequence of pre-activation values into a nonlinear pattern of outputs. Activation functions break proportionality. With a nonlinear activation function, equal changes in pre-activation values need not produce equal changes in activation values. This allows a straight-line input pattern to become a nonlinear output pattern. By adjusting the weight and bias, you will explore how different parts of that fixed function are encountered, changing the resulting activation pattern.

Think of the ground reshaping beneath the points. The observations remain the same, but their new positions relative to one another make classification easier.

Connection to neural networks: Training adjusts weights and biases, changing the pre-activation values sent to an activation function. The activation function itself usually remains fixed.
